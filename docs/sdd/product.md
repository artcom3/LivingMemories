# LivingMemories product

LivingMemories helps families preserve real testimony, language, and culture across generations. It is especially relevant to migrant families whose children grow up far from the people and places described in family memories. AI helps capture, organize, translate, and present those memories while retaining access to the people and sources behind them.

## Participants and journey

An invited adult organizes a private family. Contributors record their own or another person's testimony and review proposed adaptations. Narrators are the people to whom testimony is attributed; they may have profiles without accounts. Adult readers can accompany children or other family members. Children do not have independent pilot accounts.

Capture text, photos, recordings, or video; organize memories in albums; select existing sources; prepare a story or musical interpretation; review its draft; generate private media; read/listen; and inspect original evidence. Translation preserves the approved story rather than inventing another account. The contextual assistant answers from authorized material and distinguishes testimony from documentary context.

Guided interviews, structured fact review, final reader publication, and full retention/purge acceptance remain separate work. See the [content guide](../content-generation.md) and [assistant guide](../assistant-integration.md) for implemented workflows.

## Fidelity

Adaptation can simplify language, translate, organize testimony, and adjust detail to a reader. It must not invent scenes, exact dates, direct quotations, motivations, or happy endings. Difficult experiences need age-appropriate language without changing facts. Conflicting accounts remain distinguishable; recency or a model confidence score does not automatically resolve them.

Illustrations are artistic representations. A photograph can establish visible features but does not alone establish names, relationships, locations, or dates. Music is a creative interpretation. Neither medium becomes historical evidence because it was generated or reviewed.

## Reader profiles and pilot boundaries

The original reader presets are 2–5 years (4 pages, 80–140 words), 5–8 (6 pages, 200–350 words), 9–12 (8 pages, 450–650 words), young people (8 pages, 600–900 words), and adults (8 pages, 800–1,200 words). Adults choose a profile; overlapping ages do not imply automatic classification. Creation settings can adjust the result within implemented limits.

The planned supervised local pilot targets five families and at least twenty memories over four weeks. Completion, reliability, privacy, and fidelity targets in the [historical product definition](../history/es/docs/sdd/product.md) are goals rather than measured results. The original scope excludes child accounts, voice cloning, generated video, public memory publishing, and unrestricted web search. Assistant voice integration has its own scope and validation requirements.

English is the documentation language. Family memories and story editions retain their selected languages, including Spanish. Current translation options are documented in the [content guide](../content-generation.md).
